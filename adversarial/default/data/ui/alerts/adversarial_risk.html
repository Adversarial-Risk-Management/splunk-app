The risk title. The field accepts alert tokens, for example $name$ for the alert name or $result.hosts$ for a field of the first result. The title is a key input of AI Suggest Score. Learn more

Optional text for the top of the description. The action always adds the alert name, the trigger time and a link to the search results. The platform reads the description in AI Suggest Score, so name the affected assets and the exposure.

The category of the risk. A missing safeguard is a control deficiency. A wrong setting is a configuration risk. A known defect in third-party software is a vulnerability.
Where the risk came from. The name must exist in your organization. The risk sources and the incident sources are separate lists. To see the risk sources, open Settings > Risks in the Adversarial platform.

The status of the new risk.
The raw score of the reporting source, which is the inherent risk. "From the alert severity" maps the Splunk severity of this alert: debug and info give Info, warn gives Low, error gives Medium, severe gives High, and fatal gives Critical. AI Suggest Score reads this field when the title and the description hold too little detail.

How likely the risk is to cause a threat objective to happen.
What the risk can cause if an attacker uses it. The platform derives the urgency from the likelihood and the impact, and the due date from the urgency.

Why this likelihood applies.
Why this impact applies.

The actions that address the risk. The audience is the wider organization, so be specific.

The controls that are in place against the risk. Complete this field before the risk moves to Closed.

Fields from the first search result to add to the description. Use a comma-separated list. A name can hold a wildcard, for example host_*.

A comment gives the owner a direct link back to the Splunk results. AI Suggest Score reads the comments of a risk.
